# Topic: MC2.6 Direct & Inverse Proportion

Proportion describes the mathematical relationship between two quantities, where direct proportion satisfies \(y = kx\) and inverse proportion satisfies \(y = \frac{k}{x}\) for a constant of proportionality \(k\).

---

### 1. Proportion Types Breakdown

| Proportion Type | Proportionality Equation | Graph Characteristic | Constant \(k\) Calculation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct Proportion | \(y \propto x \implies y = kx\) | Straight line through origin \((0,0)\) | \(k = \frac{y}{x}\) |
| Inverse Proportion | \(y \propto \frac{1}{x} \implies y = \frac{k}{x}\) | Hyperbola in 1st quadrant | \(k = xy\) |

---

### 2. Anatomy of Direct vs Inverse Curves

```
       Direct Proportion (y = kx)            Inverse Proportion (y = k/x)
           y                                    y
           |   /                                |  \
           |  /                                 |   \
           | /                                  |    ` - .
           |/___ x                              +_________ x
          (0,0) Origin                             Hyperbola
```

* **Constant of Proportionality (\(k\)):** The fixed scale factor connecting two variable quantities.
* **Direct Scaling:** Doubling \(x\) doubles \(y\); inverse scaling: doubling \(x\) halves \(y\).

$$y = kx \quad \text{vs} \quad y = \frac{k}{x}$$

---

### 3. Worked Example

> **Question:**
> 1. \(y\) is directly proportional to \(x\). When \(x = 4\), \(y = 18\). Find \(y\) when \(x = 7\).
> 2. \(p\) is inversely proportional to \(q\). When \(q = 5\), \(p = 12\). Find \(p\) when \(q = 3\).

**Step 1: Calculate direct proportion constant and evaluate**

1. Set up direct equation \(y = kx\) and solve for \(k\):

$$18 = k(4) \implies k = \frac{18}{4} = 4.5$$

2. Write full formula and calculate \(y\) when \(x = 7\):

$$y = 4.5x \implies y = 4.5(7) = 31.5$$

**Step 2: Calculate inverse proportion constant and evaluate**

1. Set up inverse equation \(p = \frac{k}{q}\) and solve for \(k\):

$$12 = \frac{k}{5} \implies k = 12 \times 5 = 60$$

2. Calculate \(p\) when \(q = 3\):

$$p = \frac{60}{3} = 20$$

3. **Key result 1:** For direct proportion, \(y = 31.5\).
4. **Key result 2:** For inverse proportion, \(p = 20\).

---

### 4. Common Examiner Traps

* **Formulation Error:** Writing inverse proportion as \(y = kx\) or direct as \(y = \frac{k}{x}\).
* **Forgetting Constant Step:** Plugging values directly into ratios without establishing the constant of proportionality \(k\) first.
