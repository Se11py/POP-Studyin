# Diffusion & Osmosis Simulator — Detailed Design Specification

## I. Syllabus Alignment & Context
*   **Subject Area**: Biology
*   **Syllabus Topic Reference**: *1.3 Movement into and out of Cells*

## II. Educational Objectives
Through interaction with this visualizer, the student will:
1.  **Explain diffusion as the net movement of particles down a concentration gradient**
2.  explain osmosis as the movement of water molecules from high to low water potential through a partially permeable membrane
3.  identify factors affecting rates.
4.  Develop an intuitive understanding of the underlying principles through systematic experimentation and observation.

## III. Detailed Simulation Description
A molecular dynamics sandbox simulating random thermal particle collisions. In diffusion mode, students place a cluster of solute particles on one side and watch them diffuse evenly throughout the container. In osmosis mode, the membrane blocks solute particles but lets smaller water molecules pass. Students can observe water molecules moving towards the higher solute concentration (lower water potential) and measure the impact of temperature and concentration gradients on the rate of transport.

## IV. Simulation Interface & Interactive Controls
Students interact with the workspace using the following controls:
*   **Diffusion/Osmosis mode toggle, solute concentration slider (10 to 100 particles), solvent/water particle injector, temperature controller, membrane pore size slider, and live data graph plotter.**

## V. Dynamic Visual & Mathematical Feedback
The canvas renders the following behaviors in response to user inputs:
*   **Particle physics canvas depicting kinetic motion. Solute particles are shown in purple, water molecules in blue, and a dashed vertical line represents the membrane. Solute/water counters for the left and right chambers are updated on every frame.**

## VI. Suggested Classroom Inquiry & Laboratory Activity
1.  **Guided Inquiry (Data Collection)**: Have students run the simulation with all settings at baseline values. Instruct them to record starting measurements in a table.
2.  **Hypothesis and Testing**: Direct students to predict the impact of modifying a specific variable (e.g., doubling the mass, or halving the temperature). Let them adjust the slider and record the new output values to test their hypothesis.
3.  **Graph Analysis**: Have students note changes in the slopes or patterns of the live graphs as they vary sliders, linking geometric changes to algebraic formulas.
4.  **Synthesis**: Ask students to write down the scientific rule or mathematical formula that summarizes their findings, checking it against their textbook.
