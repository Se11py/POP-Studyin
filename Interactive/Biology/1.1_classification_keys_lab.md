# Classification & Keys Lab — Detailed Design Specification

## I. Syllabus Alignment & Context
*   **Subject Area**: Biology
*   **Syllabus Topic Reference**: *1.1 Characteristics and Classification of Living Organisms*

## II. Educational Objectives
Through interaction with this visualizer, the student will:
1.  **Understand binomial nomenclature, construct and follow dichotomous keys, and identify characteristics of the five main kingdoms (Proctotista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia, Prokaryotae).**
2.  Observe core scientific relationships dynamically.
3.  Verify mathematical equations against physical layouts.
4.  Develop an intuitive understanding of the underlying principles through systematic experimentation and observation.

## III. Detailed Simulation Description
This lab provides a sandbox for students to practice using and designing dichotomous keys. Students select an organism and follow a series of two-choice morphological questions (e.g. 'Does it have jointed legs?' -> Yes/No) to determine its genus and species. The simulation includes a designer mode where students can place organisms on a tree-graph and write their own custom keys, testing them against automatic path-finding logic.

## IV. Simulation Interface & Interactive Controls
Students interact with the workspace using the following controls:
*   **Dynamic list of organisms (insects, plants, mammals), drag-and-drop trait buckets, step-by-step branching dichotomous question prompts, and verification check button.**

## V. Dynamic Visual & Mathematical Feedback
The canvas renders the following behaviors in response to user inputs:
*   **High-fidelity vectors of organisms showing specific features (e.g. wings, jointed legs, compound eyes). As the student answers questions, relevant anatomical features are highlighted on screen to guide their observation.**

## VI. Suggested Classroom Inquiry & Laboratory Activity
1.  **Guided Inquiry (Data Collection)**: Have students run the simulation with all settings at baseline values. Instruct them to record starting measurements in a table.
2.  **Hypothesis and Testing**: Direct students to predict the impact of modifying a specific variable (e.g., doubling the mass, or halving the temperature). Let them adjust the slider and record the new output values to test their hypothesis.
3.  **Graph Analysis**: Have students note changes in the slopes or patterns of the live graphs as they vary sliders, linking geometric changes to algebraic formulas.
4.  **Synthesis**: Ask students to write down the scientific rule or mathematical formula that summarizes their findings, checking it against their textbook.
