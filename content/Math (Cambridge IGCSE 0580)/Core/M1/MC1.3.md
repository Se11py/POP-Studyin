# C1.3 Powers & Roots (Core Tier)

## Understanding Powers and Inverse Roots

A **power** (or exponent) represents repeated multiplication of a base number by itself. For example, $4^3$ means $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$.

A **root** is the exact inverse (opposite) operation of a power. Finding the square root $\sqrt{x}$ answers the question: *"What number multiplied by itself gives $x$?"* Finding the cube root $\sqrt[3]{x}$ answers the question: *"What number multiplied by itself twice gives $x$?"*

Mastering key squares and cubes up to $15^2$ and $10^3$ lets you solve non-calculator questions rapidly and simplifies algebraic equations.

## Key Rules for Evaluating Powers & Roots

1. **Fraction Square Roots**: Apply the square root to numerator and denominator separately:
   $$\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} \quad \implies \quad \sqrt{\frac{9}{25}} = \frac{\sqrt{9}}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{3}{5}$$

2. **Negative Bases with Even Exponents**: Squaring any negative number produces a **positive** result because negative times negative equals positive:
   $$(-4)^2 = (-4) \times (-4) = +16$$

3. **Negative Bases with Odd Exponents**: Cubing a negative number produces a **negative** result:
   $$(-3)^3 = (-3) \times (-3) \times (-3) = -27$$

## Step-by-Step Worked Exam Demonstrations

### Question 1: Non-Calculator Arithmetic with Powers and Roots
Evaluate the following without using a calculator:
(a) $\sqrt{144} + 3^3$  
(b) $\sqrt[3]{125} \times 2^4$  
(c) $\sqrt{\frac{49}{81}}$

#### Solution & Line-by-Line Annotations

**(a) Evaluating $\sqrt{144} + 3^3$:**
1. Evaluate $\sqrt{144}$: $12^2 = 144 \implies \sqrt{144} = 12$.
2. Evaluate $3^3$: $3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$.
3. Add the two values:
   $$12 + 27 = 39$$

**(b) Evaluating $\sqrt[3]{125} \times 2^4$:**
1. Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{125}$: $5^3 = 125 \implies \sqrt[3]{125} = 5$.
2. Evaluate $2^4$: $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 16$.
3. Multiply the two values:
   $$5 \times 16 = 80$$

**(c) Evaluating $\sqrt{\frac{49}{81}}$:**
1. Take root of numerator: $\sqrt{49} = 7$.
2. Take root of denominator: $\sqrt{81} = 9$.
3. Combine results:
   $$\sqrt{\frac{49}{81}} = \frac{7}{9}$$

## Examiner Traps & Common Candidate Pitfalls

* **Mistake: Assuming $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = a + b$**  
  *Why it happens*: Candidates incorrectly distribute the square root over addition.  
  *The Correction*: Square roots **cannot** be split over addition or subtraction! You must add the terms inside first:
  $$\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \quad (\text{NOT } 3 + 4 = 7)$$

* **Mistake: Forgetting Calculator Parentheses Around Negative Bases**  
  *Why it happens*: Entering `-3^2` into a calculator evaluates $- (3^2) = -9$.  
  *The Correction*: To square $-3$, enter `(-3)^2` with brackets to ensure the minus sign is squared ($+9$).
