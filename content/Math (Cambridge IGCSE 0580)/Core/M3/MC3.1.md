## 1. Core Intuition & Mental Model ("What is it?")

A coordinate system provides a precise two-dimensional grid to pinpoint exact locations using pairs of numbers. Think of the $x$-axis as horizontal distance along the ground (left or right) and the $y$-axis as vertical altitude (up or down). 

Every point in the Cartesian plane is written as an ordered pair $(x, y)$, where the $x$-coordinate always comes first (alphabetical order: $x$ before $y$). The intersection of the horizontal $x$-axis and vertical $y$-axis is called the **origin**, represented by $(0, 0)$.

---

## 2. Key Definitions, Principles & Formulas

### Coordinates Structure
- **Format**: $(x, y)$
  - $x$: Distance moved horizontally from the origin $(0, 0)$. Positive values mean move right; negative values mean move left.
  - $y$: Distance moved vertically from the origin $(0, 0)$. Positive values mean move up; negative values mean move down.
- **Axes**:
  - Horizontal axis: Line $y = 0$ ($x$-axis)
  - Vertical axis: Line $x = 0$ ($y$-axis)
  - Origin: $(0, 0)$

### Quadrants of the Cartesian Plane
1. **First Quadrant**: $x > 0$, $y > 0$ (Top-Right)
2. **Second Quadrant**: $x < 0$, $y > 0$ (Top-Left)
3. **Third Quadrant**: $x < 0$, $y < 0$ (Bottom-Left)
4. **Fourth Quadrant**: $x > 0$, $y < 0$ (Bottom-Right)

> 💡 **Exam Tip**: Remember the phrase *"along the corridor and up the stairs"* to ensure you always read or plot the $x$-coordinate before the $y$-coordinate.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Method & Worked Exam Demonstration

### Step-by-Step Method for Plotting & Reading Coordinates
1. **Identify the Origin $(0, 0)$**: Always begin at $(0, 0)$ when locating or plotting a point.
2. **Locate $x$-value**: Move along the horizontal $x$-axis right for positive values or left for negative values.
3. **Locate $y$-value**: From your horizontal position, move vertically up for positive values or down for negative values.
4. **Plot / Record**: Mark the point with a small neat cross $\times$ and label it clearly with its capital letter and coordinate pair $(x, y)$.

### Worked Exam Question

**Question**:
Points $A$, $B$, and $C$ are plotted on a Cartesian grid. Point $A$ has coordinates $(-3, 4)$ and point $B$ has coordinates $(5, -2)$. 

(a) Plot points $A$ and $B$ on the grid.
(b) $ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Point $C$ has coordinates $(3, -6)$. Find the coordinates of point $D$.

#### Solution & Step-by-Step Explanation:

**(a) Plotting $A(-3, 4)$ and $B(5, -2)$**:
- **Step 1**: To plot $A(-3, 4)$, start at $(0, 0)$, move $3$ units left along the $x$-axis, then $4$ units up vertically. Mark with $\times$ and label $A$.
- **Step 2**: To plot $B(5, -2)$, start at $(0, 0)$, move $5$ units right along the $x$-axis, then $2$ units down vertically. Mark with $\times$ and label $B$.

**(b) Finding the coordinates of point $D$**:
- **Step 1**: In a parallelogram $ABCD$, opposite sides are parallel and equal in length. Therefore, the vector displacement from $C$ to $D$ must equal the vector displacement from $B$ to $A$.
- **Step 2**: Calculate horizontal and vertical shift from $B(5, -2)$ to $A(-3, 4)$:
  $$\text{Change in } x = x_A - x_B = -3 - 5 = -8$$
  $$\text{Change in } y = y_A - y_B = 4 - (-2) = 6$$
- **Step 3**: Apply this exact shift to point $C(3, -6)$ to find point $D$:
  $$x_D = x_C + (-8) = 3 - 8 = -5$$
  $$y_D = y_C + 6 = -6 + 6 = 0$$
- **Step 4**: State the final coordinates clearly:
  $$D = (-5, 0)$$

## 4. Common Misconceptions & Diagnostic Exam Pitfalls

> ⚠️ **Common Misconception**: Reversing the order of coordinates, writing $(y, x)$ instead of $(x, y)$. For example, plotting $(4, -3)$ instead of $(-3, 4)$. Always check $x$ first, then $y$.

> ⚠️ **Common Misconception**: Misreading negative axes. Moving up instead of down when given a negative $y$-coordinate like $-2$.

> 💡 **Exam Tip**: When completing geometric shapes on a grid (like squares, rectangles, or parallelograms), check that opposite side lengths are equal and parallel by counting grid squares.

## 5. Key Terms & Revision Summary

| Term | Definition | Formula / Representation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cartesian Coordinates** | A pair of numerical values defining a position on a grid | $(x, y)$ |
| **$x$-axis** | The horizontal number line where $y = 0$ | Horizontal baseline |
| **$y$-axis** | The vertical number line where $x = 0$ | Vertical baseline |
| **Origin** | The central point where $x$-axis and $y$-axis cross | $(0, 0)$ |
| **Quadrant** | One of four regions divided by the axes | I: $(+,+)$, II: $(-,+)$, III: $(-,-)$, IV: $(+,-)$ |
