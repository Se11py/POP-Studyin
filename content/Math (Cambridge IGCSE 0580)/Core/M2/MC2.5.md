## 1. Core Intuition & Mental Model ("What is it?")
An equation is an algebraic balance scale asserting that two expressions have equal value. Solving an equation means determining the specific numerical value of the unknown variable that keeps the scale perfectly balanced. Rearranging a formula (changing the subject) means undoing mathematical operations in reverse order to isolate a targeted variable on one side.

Imagine an equation like a wrapped gift box. Operations (adding, subtracting, multiplying, dividing) have wrapped the unknown $x$. To solve or rearrange, you unwrap the box layer by layer using inverse operations, performing identical operations on both sides to preserve equality.

## 2. Key Definitions, Principles & Formulas
- **Linear Equation**: An equation where the highest power of the unknown variable is $1$ (e.g. $4x - 7 = 13$).
- **Subject of a Formula**: The single variable isolated on one side of an equals sign with a coefficient of $+1$ (e.g. $y$ in $y = 3x + 5$).
- **Inverse Operations**: Pairs of operations that undo each other:
  - Addition $(+)$ $\leftrightarrow$ Subtraction $(-)$
  - Multiplication $(\times)$ $\leftrightarrow$ Division $(\div)$
- **Golden Rule of Algebra**: Whatever operation is performed on one side of an equation MUST be performed on the opposite side to maintain balance.

> 💡 **Exam Tip**: When variables appear on both sides of an equation (e.g. $5x + 3 = 2x + 15$), always subtract the smaller variable term ($2x$) from both sides first to avoid negative coefficient errors.

> ⚠️ **Common Misconception**: Moving a term across the equals sign changes its sign, but dividing by a coefficient does NOT change its sign. For example, $-3x = 12 \implies x = \frac{12}{-3} = -4$.

## 3. Step-by-Step Method & Worked Exam Demonstration

### Worked Example 1: Solving Equations and Changing the Subject
(a) Solve the linear equation: $5(x - 3) = 2x + 9$.
(b) Make $h$ the subject of the formula: $V = \frac{1}{3}Ah + k$.

#### Solution:
(a)
- **Step 1: Expand the bracket on the left side of the equation.**
  $$5x - 15 = 2x + 9$$
- **Step 2: Subtract $2x$ from both sides to collect variable terms on the left.**
  $$5x - 2x - 15 = 9 \implies 3x - 15 = 9$$
- **Step 3: Add $15$ to both sides to isolate the variable term.**
  $$3x = 9 + 15 \implies 3x = 24$$
- **Step 4: Divide both sides by $3$ to find the exact value of $x$.**
  $$x = \frac{24}{3} = 8$$

(b)
- **Step 1: Subtract $k$ from both sides to isolate the term containing $h$.**
  $$V - k = \frac{1}{3}Ah$$
- **Step 2: Multiply both sides by $3$ to eliminate the fraction.**
  $$3(V - k) = Ah \implies 3V - 3k = Ah$$
- **Step 3: Divide both sides by $A$ to isolate $h$ as the subject.**
  $$h = \frac{3(V - k)}{A} \quad \text{or} \quad h = \frac{3V - 3k}{A}$$

## 4. Common Misconceptions & Diagnostic Exam Pitfalls
- **Forgetting Distributive Property**: Expanding $4(x + 2)$ to $4x + 2$ instead of $4x + 8$.
- **Division Order Inaccuracy**: In rearranging $y = mx + c$ for $x$, writing $x = \frac{y}{m} - c$ instead of first subtracting $c$: $y - c = mx \implies x = \frac{y - c}{m}$.
- **Sign Flipping Error**: Forgetting to reverse signs when moving negative terms, resulting in calculation slips.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$+a$** | $-a$ | $x + 5 = 12 \implies x = 12 - 5$ |

| **$\div a$** | $\times a$ | $\frac{x}{4} = 6 \implies x = 6 \times 4$ |
