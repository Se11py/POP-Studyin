# MC2.9 Graphs in Practical Situations | Save My Exams Revision Notes

## 1. Core Intuition & Mental Model ("What is it?")
Practical graphs display real-world quantitative relationships visually. In kinematics and real-life modeling, straight lines and curves show how two linked quantities (e.g. distance and time, or currency conversions) change relative to each other.

Think of a distance-time graph as a visual story of a journey. Time ticks forward continuously along the horizontal $x$-axis. The steepness (gradient) of the line represents how fast distance changes per unit time—in other words, the speed! A flat horizontal line means distance is not changing at all, meaning the object is stationary.

## 2. Key Definitions, Principles & Formulas
- **Gradient as Rate of Change**:
  $$\text{Gradient} = \frac{\text{Vertical Change}}{\text{Horizontal Change}} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$$
- **Distance-Time Graphs**:
  - **Gradient** = $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$
  - **Flat Horizontal Line** (gradient $= 0$): Stationary / stopped.
  - **Straight Sloped Line**: Moving at a constant speed.
  - **Steeper Slope**: Higher speed.
- **Conversion Graphs**: Straight-line graphs passing through the origin $(0,0)$ used to convert between two units (e.g. Kilograms to Pounds, Miles to Kilometers, Currency Exchange).

> 💡 **Exam Tip**: Make sure units are consistent when calculating gradients! If time on the horizontal axis is in minutes and distance is in kilometers, dividing $\frac{\text{km}}{\text{min}}$ gives speed in $\text{km/min}$. To convert to $\text{km/h}$, multiply by $60$.

> ⚠️ **Common Misconception**: On a distance-time graph, a line going downwards does NOT mean the object is moving downhill. It means the object is returning back toward its starting position.

## 3. Step-by-Step Method & Worked Exam Demonstration

### Worked Example 1: Interpreting a Distance-Time Journey Graph
A cyclist leaves home at 09:00 and travels to a village $18\text{ km}$ away.
- From 09:00 to 10:00, the cyclist travels at a constant speed, covering $18\text{ km}$.
- From 10:00 to 10:30, the cyclist stops for a rest.
- From 10:30 to 11:30, the cyclist travels back home at a constant speed.

(a) Calculate the cyclist's speed during the first hour of the journey in $\text{km/h}$.
(b) Determine the speed during the return journey back home.
(c) Calculate the total average speed for the entire trip (including rest time).

#### Solution:
(a)
- **Step 1: Identify distance traveled and time taken for the first leg.**
  $$\text{Distance} = 18\text{ km}, \quad \text{Time} = 1.0\text{ hour (09:00 to 10:00)}$$
- **Step 2: Calculate gradient (speed).**
  $$\text{Speed} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{18\text{ km}}{1.0\text{ h}} = 18\text{ km/h}$$

(b)
- **Step 1: Identify distance traveled and time taken for the return leg.**
  $$\text{Distance} = 18\text{ km}, \quad \text{Time} = 1.0\text{ hour (10:30 to 11:30)}$$
- **Step 2: Calculate return speed.**
  $$\text{Speed} = \frac{18\text{ km}}{1.0\text{ h}} = 18\text{ km/h}$$

(c)
- **Step 1: Calculate total distance covered in both directions.**
  $$\text{Total Distance} = 18\text{ km} + 18\text{ km} = 36\text{ km}$$
- **Step 2: Calculate total elapsed time from departure (09:00) to return (11:30).**
  $$\text{Total Time} = 2.5\text{ hours}$$
- **Step 3: Calculate total average speed.**
  $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{36\text{ km}}{2.5\text{ h}} = 14.4\text{ km/h}$$

## 4. Common Misconceptions & Diagnostic Exam Pitfalls
- **Confusing Time Units**: Entering $1\text{ hour } 30\text{ mins}$ into calculations as $1.3\text{ hours}$ instead of $1.5\text{ hours}$.
- **Mistaking Stationary for Zero Distance**: Interpreting a flat line at $y = 18$ as zero distance traveled, rather than staying stationary at $18\text{ km}$ from start.
- **Ignoring Rest Period in Average Speed**: Dividing total distance by moving time only, rather than total elapsed trip duration.

## 5. Key Terms & Revision Summary
| Graph Feature | Mathematical Meaning | Kinematic Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gradient ($\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$)** | Rate of change of $y$ with $x$ | Speed ($\text{m/s}$ or $\text{km/h}$) |
| **Horizontal Segment** | Zero gradient ($\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = 0$) | Stationary / stopped |
| **Positive Slope** | Positive rate of change | Moving away from start |
| **Negative Slope** | Negative rate of change | Returning toward start |
