## 1. Core Intuition & Mental Model ("What is it?")
Algebra is the language of generalisation in mathematics. Instead of working with specific fixed numbers, algebra uses letters (variables) such as $x$, $y$, and $n$ to represent unknown values or relationships that apply universally across many scenarios.

Imagine algebra as a set of labelled containers or balances. A variable like $x$ is a box holding a numerical value. An algebraic expression like $3x + 5$ represents taking three of those boxes and adding $5$ units. A formula like $A = l \times w$ acts as a universal recipe: no matter the dimensions of a rectangle, inserting the length $l$ and width $w$ into the formula outputs the exact area $A$.

## 2. Key Definitions, Principles & Formulas
- **Variable**: A letter representing an unknown quantity or a value that can change (e.g. $x$, $y$, $t$).
- **Term**: A single number, variable, or product of numbers and variables (e.g. $7$, $4x$, $-2y^2$).
- **Expression**: A mathematical phrase combining terms using addition or subtraction, without an equals sign (e.g. $5x - 3$).
- **Equation**: A mathematical statement asserting that two expressions are equal, containing an equals sign (e.g. $2x + 4 = 14$).
- **Formula**: An equation that expresses a general relationship between two or more quantities (e.g. $V = IR$).
- **Substitution**: Replacing variables with specific numerical values and evaluating the result using correct order of operations (BIDMAS).

> 💡 **Exam Tip**: When evaluating expressions with negative numbers, always place negative numbers inside parentheses before raising them to a power, e.g. $(-3)^2 = 9$, whereas $-3^2 = -9$.

> ⚠️ **Common Misconception**: Students often confuse expressions and equations. An expression like $3x + 7$ cannot be "solved" for $x$ because there is no equals sign; it can only be simplified or evaluated by substitution.

## 3. Step-by-Step Method & Worked Exam Demonstration

### Worked Example 1: Constructing Expressions and Substitution
The formula for the total cost, $C$ dollars, of renting a car for $d$ days with a fixed insurance fee of $\$25$ is given by:
$$C = 45d + 25$$

(a) Calculate the total cost $C$ when the car is rented for $d = 6$ days.
(b) Write an algebraic expression for the total cost of renting $2$ identical cars for $d$ days and purchasing $3$ child seats at $\$15$ each.

#### Solution:
(a)
- **Step 1: Substitute the value $d = 6$ into the given formula.**
  $$C = 45(6) + 25$$
- **Step 2: Perform the multiplication first according to BIDMAS rules.**
  $$C = 270 + 25$$
- **Step 3: Add the constant fee to obtain the final total cost.**
  $$C = 295$$
  The total cost is $\$295$.

(b)
- **Step 1: Write the cost expression for one car for $d$ days.**
  $$\text{Cost of 1 car} = 45d + 25$$
- **Step 2: Multiply the single car cost expression by $2$ to represent 2 cars.**
  $$\text{Cost of 2 cars} = 2(45d + 25) = 90d + 50$$
- **Step 3: Calculate the total fixed cost for $3$ child seats at $\$15$ each.**
  $$\text{Cost of 3 seats} = 3 \times 15 = 45$$
- **Step 4: Combine all terms to form the total cost expression.**
  $$\text{Total Cost} = (90d + 50) + 45 = 90d + 95$$

## 4. Common Misconceptions & Diagnostic Exam Pitfalls
- **Incorrect Order of Operations**: Performing addition before multiplication during substitution (e.g. evaluating $3 + 4 \times 2$ as $7 \times 2 = 14$ instead of $3 + 8 = 11$).
- **Omission of Parentheses in Negative Substitution**: Writing $-4^2$ on a calculator yields $-16$ instead of $(-4)^2 = 16$.
- **Conflating Terms**: Adding unlike terms together, such as simplifying $3x + 4$ to $7x$. Only like terms (terms with identical variable parts) can be combined.

## 5. Key Terms & Revision Summary
| Term | Definition & Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Expression** | A collection of terms with no equals sign (e.g. $4x - 7y$) |
| **Equation** | Two expressions set equal to solve for unknown(s) (e.g. $3x + 1 = 10$) |
| **Formula** | A rule relating multiple variables (e.g. $A = \pi r^2$) |
| **Substitution** | Replacing variables with numerical values |
| **Like Terms** | Terms containing exact same variable powers (e.g. $5ab$ and $-2ab$) |
