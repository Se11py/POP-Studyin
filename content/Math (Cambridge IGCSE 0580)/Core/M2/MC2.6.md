## 1. Core Intuition & Mental Model ("What is it?")
An inequality is a mathematical statement comparing two expressions that are not necessarily equal. Instead of finding a single numerical answer (as in an equation), solving an inequality yields a range of allowable values.

Think of an inequality like a height restriction sign for a roller coaster: "You must be at least $140\text{ cm}$ tall" translates to $h \geqslant 140$. On a number line, we represent these ranges using open circles $\circ$ for strict boundaries ($<$ or $>$) and closed circles $\bullet$ for inclusive boundaries ($\leqslant$ or $\geqslant$).

## 2. Key Definitions, Principles & Formulas
- **Inequality Symbols**:
  - $<$ : Strictly less than
  - $>$ : Strictly greater than
  - $\leqslant$ : Less than or equal to
  - $\geqslant$ : Greater than or equal to
- **Number Line Notation Rules**:
  - Open Circle ($\circ$): Used for $<$ and $>$. Boundary value is **excluded**.
  - Closed Circle ($\bullet$): Used for $\leqslant$ and $\geqslant$. Boundary value is **included**.
  - Arrow Direction: Points right for values greater than ($>$ or $\geqslant$), points left for values less than ($<$ or $\leqslant$).
- **Reversing Inequality Direction**: When multiplying or dividing both sides by a **negative number**, flip the inequality symbol ($<$ becomes $>$, $\leqslant$ becomes $\geqslant$).

> 💡 **Exam Tip**: Check your final inequality answer by substituting an easy test integer from your solution set. For example, if $x > 3$, test $x = 4$ in the original inequality.

> ⚠️ **Common Misconception**: Students often forget to flip the inequality sign when dividing by a negative coefficient. For example, $-2x < 8 \implies x > -4$.

## 3. Step-by-Step Method & Worked Exam Demonstration

### Worked Example 1: Solving Linear Inequalities & Number Line Representation
(a) Solve the inequality: $4x - 7 \leqslant 2x + 5$.
(b) Represent the solution to $-3 < x \leqslant 4$ on a number line.

#### Solution:
(a)
- **Step 1: Subtract $2x$ from both sides to collect variable terms on the left.**
  $$4x - 2x - 7 \leqslant 5 \implies 2x - 7 \leqslant 5$$
- **Step 2: Add $7$ to both sides.**
  $$2x \leqslant 5 + 7 \implies 2x \leqslant 12$$
- **Step 3: Divide both sides by positive $2$ (inequality direction stays unchanged).**
  $$x \leqslant \frac{12}{2} \implies x \leqslant 6$$

(b)
- **Step 1: Draw a straight horizontal number line marked with integers from $-4$ to $5$.**
- **Step 2: Place an OPEN circle at $-3$ because $-3 < x$ is a strict inequality (excluded).**
- **Step 3: Place a CLOSED circle at $4$ because $x \leqslant 4$ is inclusive (included).**
- **Step 4: Draw a solid horizontal line segment connecting the open circle at $-3$ to the closed circle at $4$.**

```text
    <---o===================•--->
       -3  -2  -1   0   1   2   3   4   5
```

## 4. Common Misconceptions & Diagnostic Exam Pitfalls
- **Incorrect Circle Shading**: Using a filled circle for strict inequalities ($<$ or $>$) or an open circle for inclusive inequalities ($\leqslant$ or $\geqslant$).
- **Sign Flip Omission**: Dividing $-3x \geqslant 15$ by $-3$ and writing $x \geqslant -5$ instead of flipping the sign to $x \leqslant -5$.
- **Combining Separate Statements Incorrectly**: Writing $3 > x > 7$ instead of separate statements like $x < 3$ or $x > 7$.
