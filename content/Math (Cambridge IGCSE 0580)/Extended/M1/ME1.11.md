# E1.11 Direct & Inverse Ratio & Proportion (Extended Tier)

## Advanced Proportion & Algebraic Scaling

In Extended Tier Mathematics, ratio and proportion extend into non-linear algebraic relationships involving powers ($y \propto x^2$, $y \propto x^3$) and roots ($y \propto \sqrt{x}$, $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$).

---

## Proportionality Relationships & Constant $k$

| Statement | Proportionality Symbol | Algebraic Equation | Constant $k$ Formula |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$y$ is directly proportional to $x$** | $y \propto x$ | $y = kx$ | $k = \frac{y}{x}$ |
| **$y$ is directly proportional to $x^2$** | $y \propto x^2$ | $y = kx^2$ | $k = \frac{y}{x^2}$ |
| **$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$** | $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ | $y = \frac{k}{x}$ | $k = y \cdot x$ |
| **$y$ is inversely proportional to $x^2$** | $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$ | $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ | $k = y \cdot x^2$ |
| **$y$ is directly proportional to $\sqrt{x}$** | $y \propto \sqrt{x}$ | $y = k\sqrt{x}$ | $k = \frac{y}{\sqrt{x}}$ |

---

## The 4-Step Method for Solving Proportion Problems

1. **Step 1: Write the algebraic model equation** with constant $k$ (e.g. $y = kx^2$).
2. **Step 2: Substitute given initial values** for $x$ and $y$ to solve for the numerical constant $k$.
3. **Step 3: Rewrite the explicit formula** incorporating your calculated value of $k$.
4. **Step 4: Use the formula** to calculate missing values for new inputs.

---

## Step-by-Step Worked Exam Demonstrations

### Question 1: Inverse Square Proportion
$y$ is inversely proportional to the square of $x$. Given that $y = 5$ when $x = 4$:
(a) Find an equation for $y$ in terms of $x$.  
(b) Calculate $y$ when $x = 10$.  
(c) Calculate $x$ when $y = 0.8$.

#### Solution & Line-by-Line Annotations

**(a) Finding the equation for $y$:**

1. **Step 1: Write proportionality equation**:
   $$y = \frac{k}{x^2}$$

2. **Step 2: Substitute $y = 5$ and $x = 4$**:
   $$5 = \frac{k}{4^2} \implies 5 = \frac{k}{16}$$

3. **Step 3: Solve for constant $k$**:
   $$k = 5 \times 16 = 80$$

4. **Step 4: Write final explicit formula**:
   $$y = \frac{80}{x^2}$$

**(b) Calculating $y$ when $x = 10$:**
$$y = \frac{80}{10^2} = \frac{80}{100} = 0.8$$

**(c) Calculating $x$ when $y = 0.8$:**
1. Substitute $y = 0.8$:
   $$0.8 = \frac{80}{x^2} \implies 0.8 x^2 = 80$$
2. Isolate $x^2$:
   $$x^2 = \frac{80}{0.8} = 100$$
3. Take square root ($x > 0$):
   $$x = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

## Examiner Traps & Common Candidate Pitfalls

* **Mistake: Forgetting to Power the Variable**  
  *Why it happens*: Writing $y = kx$ when the prompt specifies "proportional to the *square* of $x$".  
  *The Correction*: Always check whether the relationship involves $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, or $\sqrt{x}$!

* **Mistake: Confusing Direct and Inverse Proportion Equations**  
  *Why it happens*: Writing $y = kx^2$ for inverse proportion instead of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.  
  *The Correction*: **Direct** $\rightarrow y = k \times \text{var}$. **Inverse** $\rightarrow y = \frac{k}{\text{var}}$.
