# E1.6 Arithmetic Operations with Fractions & Negatives (Extended Tier)

## Extended Arithmetic & Fraction Mastery

In Extended Tier Mathematics, non-calculator questions test multi-step arithmetic combining mixed numbers, negative values, complex BIDMAS priorities, and algebraic fractions.

Solving these questions without a calculator requires absolute mastery of fraction reduction, common denominators via Lowest Common Multiple (LCM), and reciprocal division.

---

## Operations with Fractions Strategy Table

| Operation | Governing Mathematical Rule | Key Step | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Addition / Subtraction** | $\frac{a}{c} \pm \frac{b}{d} = \frac{ad \pm bc}{cd}$ | Convert to **Common Denominator (LCM)** | $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8+3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$ |
| **Multiplication** | $\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}$ | Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions, **cross-cancel common factors** before multiplying | $\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}$ |
| **Division** | $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$ | **Keep-Change-Flip**: multiply by reciprocal of second fraction | $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{4} = 1\frac{1}{4}$ |

## Step-by-Step Worked Exam Demonstrations

### Question 1: Non-Calculator Multi-Step Fraction Arithmetic
Show all working to evaluate the following non-calculator expression:
$$3\frac{1}{3} - 1\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{7}{8}$$

Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.

#### Solution & Line-by-Line Annotations

1. **Step 1: Identify priority operation (BIDMAS)**  
   Division takes priority over subtraction. Evaluate $1\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{7}{8}$ first.

2. **Step 2: Convert mixed number to improper fraction**:
   $$1\frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{4}$$

3. **Step 3: Execute division (Keep-Change-Flip)**:
   $$\frac{7}{4} \div \frac{7}{8} = \frac{7}{4} \times \frac{8}{7}$$
   Cancel common factor $7$ and factor $4$:
   $$\frac{1}{1} \times \frac{2}{1} = 2$$

4. **Step 4: Execute subtraction ($3\frac{1}{3} - 2$)**:
   $$3\frac{1}{3} - 2 = 1\frac{1}{3}$$

---

### Question 2: Fraction Subtraction Crossing Whole Numbers
Evaluate $4\frac{1}{5} - 2\frac{2}{3}$, giving your answer as a simplified fraction.

#### Solution & Line-by-Line Annotations

1. Convert both mixed numbers to improper fractions:
   $$4\frac{1}{5} = \frac{21}{5}, \quad 2\frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{3}$$

2. Find common denominator ($\text{LCM}(5, 3) = 15$):
   $$\frac{21 \times 3}{5 \times 3} = \frac{63}{15}, \quad \frac{8 \times 5}{3 \times 5} = \frac{40}{15}$$

3. Subtract numerators over common denominator:
   $$\frac{63}{15} - \frac{40}{15} = \frac{23}{15} = 1\frac{8}{15}$$

## Examiner Traps & Common Candidate Pitfalls

* **Mistake: Forgetting BIDMAS Priority in Mixed Expressions**  
  *Why it happens*: Evaluating $3\frac{1}{3} - 1\frac{3}{4}$ first in Question 1, then dividing by $\frac{7}{8}$.  
  *The Correction*: Division MUST be completed before subtraction!

* **Mistake: Subtracting Whole Numbers and Fractions Separately Incorrectly**  
  *Why it happens*: In $4\frac{1}{5} - 2\frac{2}{3}$, doing $(4 - 2) + (\frac{1}{5} - \frac{2}{3}) = 2 + (-\frac{7}{15})$ and getting confused by negative fractional parts.  
  *The Correction*: Always convert mixed numbers to **improper fractions** ($\frac{21}{5} - \frac{8}{3}$) before subtracting to avoid sign errors!
