# E1.5 Ordering & Real Number Inequalities (Extended Tier)

## Advanced Ordering of Complex Real Numbers

In Extended Tier Mathematics, ordering questions present complex expressions containing surds ($\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}$), negative powers ($2^{-3}$), fractional exponents ($27^{2/3}$), recurring decimals ($0.2\dot{7}$), and fundamental constants ($\pi, e$).

To arrange these expressions in ascending or descending order without error, convert all terms to **high-precision decimals (4 decimal places)** while keeping track of exact algebraic forms for your final response.

## Step-by-Step Worked Exam Demonstrations

### Question: Ordering Extended Real Quantities
Arrange the following five numbers in order of size, starting with the **smallest**:
$$16^{-1/2}, \quad \sqrt{0.2}, \quad 0.\dot{4}\dot{5}, \quad 2^{-2}, \quad \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2$$

#### Solution & Line-by-Line Annotations

1. **Convert term 1 ($16^{-1/2}$)**:
   $$16^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{16}} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.2500$$

2. **Convert term 2 ($\sqrt{0.2}$)**:
   $$\sqrt{0.2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \approx 0.4472$$

3. **Convert term 3 ($0.\dot{4}\dot{5}$)**:
   $$0.\dot{4}\dot{5} = 0.454545\dots \approx 0.4545$$

4. **Convert term 4 ($2^{-2}$)**:
   $$2^{-2} = \frac{1}{2^2} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.2500$$

5. **Convert term 5 ($\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2$)**:
   $$\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9} = 0.44444\dots \approx 0.4444$$

6. **Compare decimal magnitudes**:
   * $0.2500$ ($16^{-1/2}$ and $2^{-2}$ are equal!)
   * $0.4444$ ($\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2$)
   * $0.4472$ ($\sqrt{0.2}$)
   * $0.4545$ ($0.\dot{4}\dot{5}$)

7. **Write final ordered list using original representations**:
   $$16^{-1/2} = 2^{-2}, \quad \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2, \quad \sqrt{0.2}, \quad 0.\dot{4}\dot{5}$$

## Examiner Traps & Common Candidate Pitfalls

* **Mistake: Misestimating Square Roots of Decimals $< 1$**  
  *Why it happens*: Thinking $\sqrt{0.2} = 0.04$ or smaller than $0.2$.  
  *The Correction*: Taking the square root of a positive number **less than $1$** produces a **larger** number ($\sqrt{0.2} \approx 0.4472 > 0.2$).

* **Mistake: Equating $16^{-1/2}$ with Negative Numbers**  
  *Why it happens*: Confusing negative exponents with negative signs.  
  *The Correction*: $16^{-1/2} = +\frac{1}{4} = +0.25$, which is strictly positive!
