# Topic: MC4.1 Basic Angle Properties

Angle relationships on lines, at points, and across parallel lines are established by fundamental Euclidean theorems: angles on a straight line sum to $180^\circ$, around a point sum to $360^\circ$, and parallel transversals create equal alternate and corresponding angles.

---

### 1. Basic Angle Property Breakdown

| Angle Theorem | Geometric Setup | Angle Equation / Property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Angles on a Straight Line | Adjacent angles sharing a line vertex | $\sum \theta = 180^\circ$ |
| Angles Around a Point | Full turn around a single vertex | $\sum \theta = 360^\circ$ |
| Vertically Opposite Angles | Two intersecting straight lines | $a = b$ (equal opposite angles) |
| Alternate Angles ("Z" Angles) | Parallel lines cut by a transversal | $a = b$ (on opposite sides of transversal) |
| Corresponding Angles ("F" Angles) | Parallel lines cut by a transversal | $a = b$ (in matching relative positions) |
| Co-interior Angles ("C" Angles) | Parallel lines cut by a transversal | $a + b = 180^\circ$ (between parallel lines) |

---

### 2. Geometry & ASCII Diagram

```
                 / Transversal
         a / b  /
  --------*----/-------------------> Parallel Line 1
         c / d/
             /   e / f
  ----------/-----*----------------> Parallel Line 2
           /     g / h
          /
```

* **Alternate Angles:** Angle $c = f$ and angle $d = e$ (forms 'Z' shape between parallel lines).
* **Corresponding Angles:** Angle $a = e$, $b = f$, $c = g$, and $d = h$ (forms 'F' shape).
* **Co-interior Angles:** Angle $c + e = 180^\circ$ and angle $d + f = 180^\circ$ (forms 'C' or 'U' shape).

$$a + b = 180^\circ \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Co-interior Angles: } c + e = 180^\circ$$

---

### 3. Worked Example

> **Question:**
> Two parallel lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ are cut by a transversal. Angle $b = 115^\circ$ on $L_1$.
> 1. Calculate the magnitude of co-interior angle $e$ on $L_2$, giving a geometric reason.
> 2. Calculate the magnitude of vertically opposite angle $c$ on $L_1$.

**Step 1: Calculate Co-interior Angle $e$**

1. Identify straight-line relationship for angle $d$:

$$d = 180^\circ - b = 180^\circ - 115^\circ = 65^\circ \quad \text{(Angles on a straight line sum to } 180^\circ\text{)}$$

2. Apply co-interior angle property between parallel lines ($d + e = 180^\circ$):

$$e = 180^\circ - d = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ \quad \text{(Co-interior angles sum to } 180^\circ\text{)}$$

(Alternatively, $e = b = 115^\circ$ as alternate angles).

**Step 2: Calculate Vertically Opposite Angle $c$**

1. Apply vertically opposite angle theorem to angle $b$:

$$c = b = 115^\circ \quad \text{(Vertically opposite angles are equal)}$$

2. **Angle $e$:** $115^\circ$ (Reason: Alternate to angle $b$ OR co-interior to angle $d$)
3. **Angle $c$:** $115^\circ$ (Reason: Vertically opposite to angle $b$)

---

### 4. Common Examiner Traps

* **Omitting Geometric Reasons:** Giving the correct numerical answer (e.g. $115^\circ$) without writing the required geometric justification (e.g. "alternate angles").
* **Mislabeling "Z" and "F" Shapes:** Confusing alternate angles with corresponding angles.
* **Assuming Lines are Parallel:** Applying alternate/corresponding angle rules to lines that look parallel but lack explicit arrow markings.
